## 1. Coffee shop

A new café in a London side street.

<div className="mt-4 overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-gray-200">
  <table className="w-full text-sm">
    <tbody>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Monthly fixed costs (rent £4,000, two baristas £4,500, utilities £600, insurance £200)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">£9,300</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Selling price per coffee</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">£3.50</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Variable cost (beans, milk, cup, lid)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">£0.85</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200 bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Contribution margin per coffee</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">£2.65</td></tr>
      <tr className="bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Break-even coffees per month</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">3,510</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

3,510 / 30 = **117 coffees per day**. Over an 8-hour peak window,
that's ~15 coffees an hour, every hour. Reality check: a busy
independent café does 150–250 coffees/day; a quiet one does 50.
This shop needs to be busy, not just open.

## 2. SaaS startup

A pre-Series A SaaS product targeting small business owners.

      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Variable cost per subscriber (Stripe + support)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">$8</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200 bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Contribution per subscriber</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">$91</td></tr>

</div>

401 paying subscribers covers everything. At 5% monthly churn, the
startup needs to acquire 20+ new customers monthly just to stand
still after hitting break-even. Customer acquisition cost is
often the killer in SaaS, not the headline number.

386 bottles = 13/day. Reachable, but only with consistent ad
spend feeding the listing. Most new Amazon sellers underestimate
the ad-spend portion of variable cost.

## 4. Marketing agency

A 6-person agency selling retainer packages.

      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Variable per client (freelance contractor + tools)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">£500/mo</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200 bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Contribution per client</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">£4,000</td></tr>
      <tr className="bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Break-even clients</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">9</td></tr>

  </table>
</div>

Nine clients is the agency's threshold. Lose one, and the
gap to find a replacement determines whether next month is
profitable. Concentration risk: each client is 11%+ of revenue.

## 5. Manufacturer

A small workshop making bespoke office furniture.

<div className="mt-4 overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-gray-200">
  <table className="w-full text-sm">
    <tbody>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Monthly fixed (workshop rent, 2 craftspeople salaries, machinery lease)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">$18,500</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Average sale price per desk</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">$1,800</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200"><td className="px-4 py-2">Variable cost per desk (timber, hardware, finish, delivery)</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono">$650</td></tr>
      <tr className="border-b border-gray-200 bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Contribution per desk</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">$1,150</td></tr>
      <tr className="bg-brand-light"><td className="px-4 py-2 font-semibold">Break-even desks per month</td><td className="px-4 py-2 text-right font-mono font-semibold">17</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

17 desks/month — about one every working day. Each craftsperson
produces 8–10 desks/month, so two craftspeople = capacity for
about 18, which only just covers break-even. Adding a third
craftsperson would lift fixed costs but allow real profit.

## What these examples have in common

- **Most are tighter than they look on paper.** Break-even is the floor — real businesses need a buffer above it.
- **Concentration risk shows up in low-unit-count businesses.** 9 agency clients or 17 desks/month means losing one customer hurts a lot.
- **Variable costs are usually under-counted.** First-time founders forget platform fees, ad spend, payment processing, and returns.
